Forming an LLC comes down to three paths: do it yourself through the state, pay a formation service like BusinessAnywhere, or hire an attorney. DIY is cheapest; the question is what you’re actually buying when you pay someone else. Here’s the honest breakdown.

The DIY Path: What It Really Involves

DIY LLC filing means going directly to your Secretary of State’s website, completing the articles of organization, paying the state filing fee, and then handling everything else yourself: appointing a registered agent (legally required in every state — you can serve as your own in most states if you have an in-state address and are available during business hours), obtaining an EIN from the IRS (free, but the application has its own quirks), drafting an operating agreement, opening a business bank account, and calendaring annual reports and compliance deadlines.

The cost is just the state fees — the cheapest possible route. The price is your time and your error surface. Common DIY mistakes: filing in the wrong state (hint: for most small businesses, it’s your home state, not Delaware), skipping the operating agreement, forgetting the EIN before opening the bank account, and missing the first annual report because nobody told you it existed. None are fatal; all are annoying, and some cost money to fix.

What BusinessAnywhere Adds on Top

BusinessAnywhere (businessanywhere.io) wraps the filing in a guided online process and bundles the satellite services: registered agent service, a virtual mailbox with a real US street address (their FAQ honestly discloses it can’t substitute for the registered agent address), EIN/ITIN assistance, introductions to banking partners for fully online US business account opening, bookkeeping and tax filing support, and virtual notary services. Expedited filing options can shorten formation to as little as ~2 business days in certain states, per the brand.

The headline is “start your company today for $0” — the service-fee starting tier, with state filing fees on top. Formation services monetize through tier upsells and renewals (registered agent, compliance packages, mail plans), so the comparison that matters is total first-year cost, not the headline.

The real product isn’t the filing — it’s error prevention and consolidation. One provider for formation, agent, mailbox, EIN guidance, and banking introductions versus five separate vendors you research yourself. For international founders, the bundled guidance through EIN and banking — the two steps that most often stall non-resident formations — is the substantive differentiator, and the brand’s FAQ explicitly builds the flow around them.

Head-to-Head: Cost, Time, and Risk

Cost: DIY wins outright — state fees only. BusinessAnywhere adds a service fee plus any recurring add-ons. An attorney costs $1,000+ for formation alone. If budget is the only variable, DIY is the answer.

Time: DIY costs hours of research plus the filing itself, spread across the satellite tasks (EIN, agent, bank account) over days or weeks. BusinessAnywhere compresses this into one guided flow. Value your time honestly: if five hours of administrative research costs you five billable hours, the service fee may pay for itself.

Error risk: DIY’s risk is concentrated in the things first-timers don’t know — operating agreements, compliance calendars, registered agent requirements, state selection. A formation service’s checklists and guidance eliminate most of these. Neither DIY nor a formation service substitutes for an attorney when the situation is complex.

Ongoing compliance: This is underrated. DIY leaves you to build your own reminder system for annual reports and renewals; formation services typically include compliance reminders or managed filings. Entities that lapse administratively are a quiet, common, expensive problem.

When Each Path Wins

DIY wins when: the formation is simple (single-member LLC, home state, no international complications), you’re comfortable with government portals and paperwork, and your time is currently cheaper than the service fee. Plenty of straightforward LLCs are formed this way every day.

BusinessAnywhere wins when: you want the satellite services bundled (agent, mailbox, EIN help, banking), you’re a non-US founder navigating the EIN/banking maze, you’d rather pay than research, or you value having compliance reminders handled. The middle path is also the right one when “cheap but guided” beats both extremes.

An attorney wins when: the situation is legally complex — multi-member operating agreements with unusual terms, regulated industries, intricate tax situations, or significant liability exposure. No formation wizard substitutes for counsel here, and pretending otherwise is how expensive mistakes happen.

The Bottom Line

DIY filing is cheapest and perfectly fine for simple formations by people comfortable with paperwork. BusinessAnywhere earns its fee through bundling and guidance — registered agent, mailbox, EIN, banking, compliance — with particular value for international founders and anyone who’d rather pay than research five separate vendors. Attorneys remain the answer for complex situations. Pick based on your complexity and how you value your time, price the full first year including renewals, and don’t skip the operating agreement whichever path you choose.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to file an LLC yourself? Yes — DIY filing costs only the state filing fees, making it the cheapest option by definition. What you give up is guidance: no checklists for the operating agreement, EIN process, or compliance calendar, and no bundled registered agent or mailbox. Whether the savings are worth it depends on your comfort with paperwork and how you value your time.

What does BusinessAnywhere’s “$0” formation actually include? The “$0” refers to the starting service-fee tier — state filing fees are always separate and vary by state. The service wraps the filing in a guided process and offers bundled add-ons: registered agent, virtual mailbox, EIN/ITIN help, banking introductions, bookkeeping, tax support, and notary services. Confirm the total first-year cost including renewals before committing.

Do I need a registered agent if I form an LLC myself? Yes — every formation state legally requires a registered agent: someone with an in-state address available during business hours to receive government notices and legal documents. You can usually serve as your own if you qualify, or use a service like BusinessAnywhere’s. A virtual mailbox address generally cannot substitute for the registered agent address.

When should I hire a business attorney instead? When the situation is legally complex: multi-member operating agreements with unusual terms, regulated industries, intricate tax situations, or significant liability exposure. Formation services handle straightforward filings well, but they’re document processors, not counsel — and the cost of getting complex formation wrong dwarfs the attorney’s fee.
