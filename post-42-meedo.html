Meedo Designs Rooftop Tents: An Honest Look at the Apolle Line

Sleeping on the ground in a tent you have to pitch every night gets old fast — especially when the ground is rocky, wet, or home to things that crawl. Rooftop tents fix that: your shelter lives on your vehicle’s roof, sets up in minutes, and you sleep on a built-in mattress above it all. The problem is the price — the big names charge $2,000 to $4,000 for the privilege.

Meedo Designs makes soft-shell rooftop tents at noticeably lower prices, with their Apolle line as the flagship. The Apolle TNT L fits 3 people at around $1,499, and the Apolle A86 M fits 2 at roughly $999.99. That undercuts premium brands by a lot. The question is whether a budget rooftop tent is actually a smart buy.

What Meedo Designs Actually Is

Meedo Designs is the consumer brand of the Meedo Group, a Chinese manufacturer specializing in car outdoor equipment and rooftop tents since 2014. The company operates ISO 9001 and BSCI quality management systems and sells direct, which is how they keep prices down versus Western brands.

The Apolle line is soft-shell rooftop tents — the fold-out style with a fabric rainfly rather than a hard plastic shell. Key specs from real listings: the Apolle TNT L is a 3-person tent with a 4000mm waterproof rating, aluminum-alloy frame components, a bi-fold clamshell design, and an included ladder; the Apolle A86 M is a 2-person model with a waterproof rainfly-fold design, also with ladder included. Both mount to roof racks on SUVs, trucks, Jeeps, and vans. Prices spotted: ~$1,499 for the TNT L and ~$999.99 for the A86 M — check the current price on their site, as rooftop tent pricing shifts with shipping costs.

Who It’s For — and Who Should Skip It

It’s for you if: you want a rooftop tent but can’t justify $2,500+ for a premium brand; you camp a handful of weekends a year rather than full-time overland; you drive an SUV, truck, or Jeep with a rated roof rack and crossbars already installed; or you’re outfitting a budget overland build and want to spend the savings on other gear.

Skip it if: you’re planning a months-long overland expedition through serious weather — the big brands earn their prices in durability under daily abuse. Skip it if your vehicle lacks a proper roof rack (the tent is only as safe as its mounting), and skip it if you’re buying from overseas shipping without checking the warranty and support terms — a budget tent with no support is a gamble. Also: rooftop tents are heavy (typically 120+ lbs) and reduce fuel economy, so if that’s a concern, a ground tent is still the cheaper, lighter answer.

Key Features Worth Knowing

  • 4000mm waterproof rating: A genuinely respectable waterproofing spec for the price tier — most weather except true downpours is handled.
  • 2- and 3-person options: The A86 M suits couples; the TNT L’s bi-fold design opens wider for a family of three — pick based on who’s actually sleeping in it.
  • Aluminum alloy frame: Lighter and more corrosion-resistant than cheap steel hardware, which matters for something living on your roof.
  • Ladder included: Not every budget tent includes the ladder — here it’s in the box, so there’s no hidden extra purchase.
  • ISO 9001 manufacturing: Factory-level quality certification doesn’t guarantee a perfect product, but it’s more reassurance than most budget tents offer.

Price and Value

This is where Meedo wins. At roughly $1,000–$1,500, these tents cost about half what a Roofnest or iKamper charges for a comparable soft-shell. For a weekend camper, that math is compelling: you’re getting a functional rooftop tent for the price of a high-end ground tent plus a nice mattress.

The honest trade-off is what you give up at this price: no US-based support network, uncertain long-term parts availability, and reviews from a smaller user base than the established brands. The value case is strong for occasional campers; weaker if you need a tent that survives daily use for years. And always confirm the current price and shipping terms on their site before ordering — freight costs on a 120+ lb tent can change the equation.

The Bottom Line

Meedo Designs offers the cheapest credible path into rooftop tent camping: real waterproof specs, included ladders, aluminum construction, and prices around half of the big brands. It’s not an expedition-grade tent, and you should know exactly what support you’re getting before you buy — but for weekend adventurers who want off-the-ground comfort without the premium price, the Apolle line is worth a serious look.
