When to Replace Your Mattress: 9 Signs It's Time for a New One

Most people keep their mattress years longer than they should. It’s not because they’re cheap — it’s because mattress decline is gradual. You don’t wake up one day on a terrible mattress; you slowly stop sleeping as well as you used to, and you blame everything else first: stress, the pillow, your schedule, getting older.

This guide covers the real signs your mattress is done, the rough lifespan to expect from different mattress types, and what to do when you’re not sure.

How long do mattresses actually last?

There’s no single expiration date, but here’s the honest range most sleepers experience:

  • Innerspring: roughly 7–8 years before coils wear and comfort layers compress
  • Memory foam: roughly 8–10 years, depending on foam density (higher-density foams hold up longer)
  • Hybrid: roughly 8–10 years — the coil base tends to outlast the comfort layers
  • Latex: often 10–12+ years; latex is the durability champion of mattress materials

These are averages, not guarantees. A heavier sleeper, a mattress on a bad foundation, or one that’s never rotated will wear faster. Read our mattress types comparison if you’re weighing what to buy next.

9 signs your mattress needs replacing

1. You wake up sore or stiff — and it fades during the day

Morning back, neck, or shoulder pain that eases within an hour or two of getting up is the classic worn-mattress signature. Your mattress is no longer supporting your spine’s alignment overnight.

2. There’s a visible sag or dip

Strip the sheets and look at the sleep surface from the side. If there’s a visible valley where you sleep — typically around the hips — the support layers have failed. Note: most warranties only cover sags deeper than 1–1.5 inches, so a visible-but-shallow dip usually isn’t a warranty claim.

3. You sleep better anywhere else

If you consistently sleep better in hotels, at a friend’s place, or even on the couch, your mattress is telling you something. This is one of the most reliable tests there is.

4. It makes noise

Creaking, groaning, or popping when you move usually means the coil system is wearing out. A little noise isn’t an emergency, but new or worsening noise after years of quiet is a wear signal.

5. You feel your partner’s every move

Motion isolation degrades as materials break down. If you used to sleep through your partner turning over and now every shift wakes you, the comfort layers have lost their ability to absorb movement.

6. It’s lumpy or uneven

Run your hand across the surface. Lumps, ridges, or areas that feel noticeably softer than others mean the fill or foam layers have shifted or compressed unevenly.

7. Your allergies are worse in bed

Old mattresses accumulate dust mites, dead skin, and allergens over the years — a protector slows this but doesn’t stop it. If you wake up congested or sneezy and it improves once you’re up, an aging mattress may be contributing.

8. You’re rolling toward the middle (or your partner)

That slow drift toward the center of the bed is edge support and center support breaking down. Couples notice this first — suddenly you’re both in the middle.

9. It’s simply old

If your mattress is past the lifespan range for its type and you’re sleeping worse than you used to, you don’t need another sign. Age plus declining sleep quality is enough.

Can a topper buy you more time?

A quality mattress topper can extend a mattress’s useful life by a year or two if the problem is surface comfort — a too-firm feel or minor pressure points. It cannot fix a sagging support core. If the mattress dips or your spine is misaligned, a topper just puts a comfortable layer on top of a broken foundation. It’s a bridge to a new mattress, not a replacement for one.

What to do with the old one

  • Donate if it’s still in decent shape — many charities accept clean, undamaged mattresses.
  • Recycle — mattress recycling programs exist in many areas and keep bulky materials out of landfills.
  • Retailer haul-away — many mattress sellers remove your old one for free or a small fee with delivery.

FAQ

How often should you replace your mattress? Most sleepers should plan on replacing every 7–10 years, depending on the mattress type and how it’s held up. Replace sooner if you notice the signs above.

Does flipping or rotating help? Rotating (head to foot) every 6–12 months evens out wear on most modern mattresses. Most one-sided mattresses should not be flipped — check your manufacturer’s guidance.

Is an old mattress bad for your back? A worn mattress that no longer supports proper spinal alignment can contribute to morning stiffness and back discomfort. If pain fades after you’re up and moving, the mattress is a likely culprit — though it’s not medical advice, and persistent pain deserves a professional’s opinion.

Can I just replace the box spring? If the foundation is the problem — broken slats, a sagging box spring — replacing it can genuinely help. But if the mattress itself is worn, a new foundation won’t save it.
