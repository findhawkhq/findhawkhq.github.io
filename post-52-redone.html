Re/Done Jeans: Worth the Premium? An Honest Look

2026-10-25

Jeans are jeans — until they’re $265+. Re/Done is the LA brand that made “repurposed vintage Levi’s, deconstructed and resewn” into a luxury category, hand-cut from actual vintage denim and sold at designer prices.

The pitch is seductive: a pair of jeans nobody else owns, made from denim with decades of natural wear that no factory distressing can copy. But the price is real, the fit quirks are real, and so is the question of whether any jeans are worth designer money.

What Re/Done Actually Is

Re/Done (stylized RE/DONE) is a Los Angeles-based premium denim label. Its signature product is jeans made from repurposed vintage Levi’s: the company sources vintage Levi’s as raw goods, sorts them one by one, washes them, takes them apart completely at the seams, then lays the pieces against modern patterns, cuts them, and sews them back together.

The result is that every pair is genuinely one of a kind. The wash, fading, whiskering, and distressing are the real thing — decades of actual wear — not factory-applied techniques. Expect slight variations between what you see pictured and what arrives.

The lineup includes repurposed vintage styles (like the High Rise Stovepipe) as well as an “Originals” line made from new stretch denim for dark indigo shades and more comfort — the brand’s answer to customers who wanted the fit without rigid vintage fabric. They also make denim shorts and jackets.

Price-wise, jeans typically run in the mid-$200s to high-$300s — Nordstrom currently lists styles around $265, with some pairs near $398 on resale listings, and shorts historically starting around $182. Prices move with collections and sales, so check the current price on their site or a retailer before buying.

Who It’s For — and Who Should Skip It

It’s for you if: You’re a denim person who cares about wash and character. If you’ve ever hunted thrift stores for the perfect vintage Levi’s with natural fading, Re/Done is that hunt done for you — with a modern, tailored fit. The authentic wear is the whole point, and you can’t get it from new denim at any price.

It’s for you if: Sustainability matters in your fashion choices. No new denim is dyed or distressed with harsh chemicals for the repurposed line — the wear is real. Upcycling vintage Levi’s is meaningfully more circular than buying new fast-fashion jeans.

It’s for you if: You want premium denim that isn’t stiff and precious. Re/Done’s fits are relaxed, cropped, frayed-hem, ’90s-inspired — jeans that look better lived-in.

Skip it if: You’re sensitive about fit consistency. This is the big one. Because each pair is cut from different vintage jeans, fit can genuinely vary — Nordstrom’s own size guidance suggests considering one size up, and the rigid vintage fabric has little to no stretch. If you hate the gamble of online sizing, try them in a store if you can.

Skip it if: You need stretch and all-day comfort. The repurposed vintage line is 100% cotton, rigid denim — no give. If you live in stretch jeans, the Originals line (cotton/polyester/elastane blend) is the better option, but at that point you’re paying Re/Done prices for non-vintage denim, which undercuts the brand’s main selling point.

Skip it if: The price makes you wince for jeans. Be honest: $265+ is a lot for denim, and the quality-of-life improvement over a good $80 pair is mostly aesthetic, not functional.

Key Features Worth Knowing

  • Genuinely repurposed vintage Levi’s: Deconstructed, washed, recut, and resewn in Los Angeles. Every pair is one of a kind with natural fading, whiskering, and distressing.
  • No harsh chemicals or artificial distressing: The brand doesn’t use dyes or distressing chemicals for the vintage line — the wear is real, which is both a sustainability story and an aesthetic one.
  • Handmade construction: Small-batch, hand-cut, and sewn, with measured checks at each phase of production for fit consistency.
  • An “Originals” line for stretch lovers: New stretch denim in deep indigo washes with modern fits (high rise, raw cropped hems) for buyers who want the Re/Done look with comfort.
  • Iconic fits: High Rise Stovepipe, cropped frayed hems, ’90s-inspired straight legs — the fits that made the brand famous, plus shorts and denim jackets.

Price and Value

Re/Done jeans are expensive because of process and positioning, not magical fabric. You’re paying for the sourcing, the deconstruction, the handwork in LA, the one-of-a-kind factor, and the brand. Compared to other designer denim ($250-$400), the price is in line. Compared to actual thrift-store vintage Levi’s ($20-$60), you’re paying a big premium for curation and modern fit.

The value case: if you love authentic vintage character, want a modern fit, and would rather not spend weekends digging through thrift racks, the premium buys you exactly that. The Originals line is harder to justify — at similar prices, you’re paying designer money for new stretch denim with plenty of options at half the price.

Watch for sales: Re/Done styles do get marked down at retailers — 40% off isn’t unusual on past-season washes — and that’s where the value gets much stronger.

The Bottom Line

Re/Done is the real deal for what it claims to be: authentic repurposed vintage Levi’s, rebuilt into modern designer fits, with genuinely one-of-a-kind character. The premium is real, the fit gamble is real, and rigid vintage fabric isn’t for everyone. But if you value authentic wear and sustainable upcycling, this is one of the few brands where the story on the label is actually true.

Just buy the vintage line for the vintage story — and size up if you’re between sizes.
