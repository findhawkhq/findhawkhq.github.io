Purple vs Helix: Gel Grid vs Hybrid — Which Sleeps Better?

Purple and Helix get compared constantly, but they’re selling fundamentally different ideas about comfort.

Purple and Helix get compared constantly, but they’re selling fundamentally different ideas about comfort. Purple’s entire identity is the GelFlex Grid — a layer of hyper-elastic polymer shaped into a grid that collapses under pressure points and stays supportive everywhere else. Helix’s identity is personalization — a big lineup of hybrid mattresses tuned for different sleep positions and body types, matched to you through a sleep quiz.

One is a materials innovation. The other is a fitting system.

The real question isn’t “which brand is better” — it’s whether you want Purple’s unique grid feel or Helix’s tailored-to-you hybrid approach. They barely overlap.

How Purple Mattresses Are Built

Purple’s GelFlex Grid is genuinely different from anything else in the mainstream mattress market. It’s a slab of hyper-elastic polymer — a rubbery, stretchy material — molded into a grid of hollow columns. Under light pressure (your broad back), the columns hold firm. Under concentrated pressure (your shoulders, hips), the columns buckle and collapse, letting those points sink in.

What that means in practice:

  • Pressure relief without the sink. The grid cradles pressure points while the rest of you stays supported.
  • Instantly responsive. The polymer snaps back immediately. Changing position is effortless — combination sleepers tend to love this.
  • Airflow by design. The grid is mostly open space, so air moves through it freely. Purple’s flagship sleeps noticeably cooler than all-foam mattresses as a category.
  • A polarizing feel. This is the thing reviews dance around: the grid feels weird at first. Some people love it immediately; others need the trial period to decide. It’s not a subtle difference — it’s a different sensation from foam entirely.

Purple’s lineup: the flagship Purple Mattress (grid over foam base), the Restore line (grid over pocketed coils — a true hybrid), and premium tiers with thicker grids.

Purple generally advertises around a 100-night trial and a 10-year warranty.

How Helix Mattresses Are Built

Helix doesn’t have a signature material — it has a signature system. You take a sleep quiz (position, body type, firmness preference, temperature), and Helix matches you to one of its many hybrid models. The lineup is organized around who you are rather than around a technology.

The core lineup generally includes:

  • Core models tuned for specific sleepers — e.g., softer models for side sleepers, firmer for back and stomach sleepers.
  • Luxe and Elite tiers with upgraded coils, more cooling features, and plusher covers at higher prices.
  • Specialty models, including options marketed toward plus-size sleepers with reinforced support.

Every Helix is a hybrid: memory foam or polyfoam comfort layers over individually wrapped pocketed coils. That’s a proven, familiar construction — contouring on top, bounce and airflow underneath.

What the Helix approach means in practice:

  • A model for your specifics. Side sleeper with shoulder pain? There’s a softer Helix for that. Stomach sleeper? There’s a firmer one. The quiz does the narrowing for you.
  • Familiar feel. If you’ve slept on a decent pillow-top hybrid, a Helix will feel like home. No learning curve, no weird first impression.
  • Good all-around performance. Hybrids are the jack-of-all-trades construction: decent cooling, decent motion isolation, decent edge support, decent responsiveness.

Helix generally advertises around a 100-night trial and a 10- to 15-year warranty depending on the model tier.

Head-to-Head: The Differences That Matter

Feel and firmness

This is the whole comparison. Purple feels like nothing else — buoyant, floaty, instantly responsive, with pressure relief that doesn’t trap you. Helix feels like a very good conventional hybrid — contouring foam, gentle bounce from the coils, familiar and predictable.

  • Choose Purple if you’re intrigued by the grid and want maximum responsiveness. Combination sleepers who toss and turn often prefer it.
  • Choose Helix if you want a known quantity, or if you’ve tried Purple-style feels and didn’t like them. There’s no substitute for “feels like a normal nice mattress.”

Firmness: Purple’s flagship sits around medium-firm. Helix offers actual firmness options across its lineup — soft, medium, firm. If you know you need something softer or firmer than average, Helix’s range is the advantage.

Pressure relief

Both do well, differently. Purple’s grid is excellent at pressure relief for back sleepers and many side sleepers — the buckling columns are genuinely effective at hips and shoulders. Helix’s softer models (aimed at side sleepers) also relieve pressure well through thicker foam comfort layers.

Cooling

Purple has the edge. The open grid structure moves air far better than solid foam layers, and Purple’s flagship sleeps cooler than most foam hybrids. Helix’s standard models sleep temperature-neutral for hybrids — fine, not remarkable — though the higher Luxe/Elite tiers add cooling features that close the gap. If you sleep hot and you’re choosing between the base models, Purple is the cooler pick.

Motion isolation

Helix’s all-foam-over-coil hybrids isolate motion well — pocketed coils move independently, and the foam layers absorb the rest. Purple’s grid is responsive by design, which means it transmits a bit more movement than deep memory foam, though it’s still far better than a traditional innerspring. Light sleepers with restless partners may slightly prefer Helix.

Edge support

Helix wins clearly. Pocketed coil units with reinforced perimeters give Helix models solid, sit-able edges. If edge support matters to you — you sit on the edge, sleep near the edge, or need it for mobility — Helix, or Purple’s Restore line specifically.

Durability expectations

The GelFlex Grid is a durable material by nature — hyper-elastic polymer doesn’t take a compression set the way foam does, so the comfort layer itself should outlast equivalent foam. Helix’s hybrids use standard-quality foams over coils; expect the typical 7-to-10-year hybrid lifespan with gradual softening of the comfort layers. Neither brand is a durability risk; Purple’s grid is arguably the longer-lived comfort material of the two.

Trial, warranty, and returns

Both generally advertise around 100-night trials — enough time to get past the break-in period (give any mattress at least 30 nights). Purple’s trial matters more in practice, because the grid feel is polarizing: you genuinely may need 3–4 weeks to decide if you like it. Warranties are comparable (around 10 years), with the usual conditions about sag depth and foundations.

Price and value

Purple’s flagship typically costs more than Helix’s core models — you’re paying for the proprietary grid. Helix’s quiz-matched core models are often the better pure value: a well-fitted hybrid for a mid-range price. Purple justifies its premium only if you actually prefer the grid feel — which is exactly what the trial is for.

Which Should You Buy?

Choose Purple if: - You sleep hot — the grid’s airflow is a real advantage - You’re a combination sleeper who changes position and hates feeling “stuck” - You’re a back sleeper wanting pressure relief without deep sink - You’re curious about the grid and willing to use the trial to decide

Choose Helix if: - You want a mattress matched to your sleep position and body type - You prefer a familiar foam-and-coil feel with no learning curve - Edge support matters to you - You want strong value in a hybrid — the core models are priced to compete

Look elsewhere if: you want classic slow-sink memory foam (neither brand specializes in it), you’re on a tight budget (both sit above the budget tier), or you weigh over 230 pounds and need reinforced construction (Helix has plus-size options; Purple’s standard models aren’t built for it).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Purple grid really feel that different?

Yes — and this is the least controversial thing reviewers say about it. It’s buoyant and springy rather than contouring and slow. Most people either love it within a week or decide it’s not for them. The 100-night trial exists precisely because of this.

Is Helix good for couples with different sleep needs?

It’s one of the better setups for that: each partner takes the quiz and can be matched to different models, including split-firmness options on some configurations. Purple’s one-feel-fits-most grid is less accommodating when partners disagree.

Which is better for back pain?

It depends on the cause, but both can work. Purple’s grid keeps the spine aligned while relieving pressure points — many back sleepers like it. Helix’s medium-firm models matched to back sleeping do the same job conventionally. Our guide to mattresses for back pain covers the firmness research behind both approaches.

Do I have to take Helix’s quiz?

No, but it’s the point of the brand. You can browse models directly by firmness and position. The quiz just saves you the homework.

Can I try Purple in a store?

Purple has retail partnerships and showrooms in many areas — worth doing, since the grid feel is the whole decision. Helix is primarily online, like most bed-in-a-box brands.

The Bottom Line

Purple is the interesting choice: a genuinely novel comfort material with real cooling and responsiveness advantages, at a premium price, with a feel you’ll either love or return. Helix is the sensible choice: a well-executed personalized hybrid system that gets most sleepers to the right firmness with minimal fuss, at a competitive price.

If you’re excited by the grid, try Purple — the trial makes the experiment risk-free. If you just want the right mattress for your body with the least drama, take Helix’s quiz.
