Gourmet dog gifts — treats that look like they came from a Parisian pâtisserie — solve the problem elegantly. Here’s why Bonne et Filou’s dog macarons top our 2026 list.

Why Gourmet Treats Beat Toys as Gifts

Dog toys are a saturated gift category: the recipient’s dog likely has a toy basket overflowing with half-chewed plushies, and a new one blends into the pile by New Year’s. Edible gifts don’t have that problem — they’re consumed, appreciated, and gone, leaving no clutter. More importantly, a beautiful box of dog macarons signals thoughtfulness in a way a rawhide bone never will.

There’s also the “gift for the human” factor. Let’s be honest: most pet gifts are really for the owner. A box of French-pastry-inspired dog macarons delights the dog person unwrapping it — it’s photogenic, unexpected, and fun to share on social media. The press badges on Bonne et Filou’s site (Oprah’s Favorite Things, Vogue, Cosmopolitan, Marie Claire, per the brand) tell you exactly which audience this lands with: people who appreciate presentation.

What Bonne et Filou Offers for Holiday Gifting

The gifting lineup centers on the signature dog macarons — sandwich-style treats modeled on French pastry, which the brand states are handmade in North America from a 100% natural recipe with human-grade ingredients, free of corn, wheat, artificial coloring, and preservatives. Seven flavors (Strawberry, Vanilla, Pumpkin, Peanut Butter, Birthday Cake, Crème Brûlée, Cheese) come in various multi-packs and gift sets, so you can scale from a stocking stuffer to a statement gift.

Beyond treats, the brand sells plush toys made from what they describe as ultra-soft, hypoallergenic recycled materials with concealed squeakers — a natural add-on that turns a treat box into a full gift bundle. There’s also a subscription plan for the dog lover in your life who’d enjoy repeat deliveries, and an advent calendar product that is frankly perfect for December gifting.

Gift Ideas by Recipient

For the dog mom/dad who has everything: A curated macaron gift set in the full flavor range. It’s the one dog gift they almost certainly don’t own yet, and the unboxing experience is half the present.

For the new puppy’s family: A smaller multi-pack plus one of the plush toys. Keep it modest — new puppy households are drowning in stuff, and a tasteful small gift beats a huge one.

For the picky dog: The savory flavors (Peanut Butter, Cheese) in a small pack. Thoughtful because it shows you actually considered the dog, not just the aesthetic.

For the dog-obsessed coworker or neighbor: The advent calendar. Seasonal, fun, shareable, and it keeps giving through December.

For your own dog: The subscription plan — a gift to yourself that keeps your treat jar stocked with something better than grocery-store biscuits.

Timing and Ordering Tips

Holiday shipping is the unglamorous part of gift guides, and it matters here more than usual: these are food products, and you want them arriving fresh and well before Christmas, not in the December 23rd delivery chaos. Order early — mid-November is the safe window — and check the current price on gift sets, since the brand tends to run seasonal promotions and bundle deals around the holidays.

If you’re shipping directly to the recipient, confirm the packaging is gift-ready (most of Bonne et Filou’s gift sets are designed for exactly this). And if the recipient’s dog has known food allergies, do the considerate thing and check the ingredient panel first — “natural” and “human-grade” are the brand’s claims, not a guarantee of allergen-free.
