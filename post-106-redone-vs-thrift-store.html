Re/Done vs Thrift Vintage: The Honest Math

Here’s the uncomfortable question behind Re/Done’s $265+ price tag: the raw material is literally old Levi’s — the same jeans sitting in thrift stores for $20 to $60.

Here’s the uncomfortable question behind Re/Done’s $265+ price tag: the raw material is literally old Levi’s — the same jeans sitting in thrift stores for $20 to $60. So what exactly are you paying a 5x to 10x premium for? Curation? Craft? Or just a brand name on someone else’s old jeans?

It’s a fair challenge, and the answer is more interesting than either side admits. Here’s the honest math. See our full Re/Done review for the brand background.

What the Thrift Store Gives You

Let’s steelman the thrift case, because it’s strong. For $20–$60, you get genuinely vintage Levi’s with real decades-old wear — the exact same raw material Re/Done starts with. The fading, whiskering, and distressing are authentic because they’re actual history. No factory distressing, no harsh chemicals, fully circular. On material authenticity alone, the thrift store ties Re/Done at a tenth of the price.

What you don’t get: the fit. Vintage Levi’s were cut for bodies and tastes of past decades — high rises that aren’t quite right, legs that need tailoring, sizes that ran differently. You get the hunt, which is fun until it’s your fourth Saturday digging through racks. And you get zero quality control: blown-out knees, mystery stains, and washes that look better on the hanger than on you.

What Re/Done Actually Does to the Jeans

This is the part the “it’s just old jeans” dismissal misses. Re/Done doesn’t resell vintage Levi’s — they deconstruct them. The company sources vintage Levi’s as raw goods, sorts them one by one, washes them, takes them completely apart at the seams, lays the pieces against modern patterns, cuts them, and sews them back together in Los Angeles, with measured checks at each phase.

The result: vintage character with a modern, tailored fit — the High Rise Stovepipe, cropped frayed hems, ’90s-inspired straight legs. That’s the product. You’re not paying $265 for old jeans; you’re paying for the hunt done for you, the deconstruction and reconstruction labor, handmade small-batch construction, and a fit designed for right now instead of 1987.

The Honest Math

Buy thrift if: you enjoy the hunt, you know your way around a tailor (or like the raw vintage fit as-is), your budget is real, and you don’t mind the gamble. $20–$60 for authentic vintage denim is unbeatable value, full stop. Nobody who loves thrifting needs Re/Done.

Buy Re/Done if: you want the vintage look with a modern fit and you’d rather not spend weekends digging. The premium buys curation (someone sorted thousands of pairs so you didn’t have to), reconstruction labor in LA, and fit consistency checks. Against other designer denim at $250–$400, the price is in line — you’re just getting vintage character instead of new-denim prestige.

The sale caveat: Re/Done styles get marked down at retailers — 40% off past-season washes isn’t unusual. At $159 on sale, the math against $40 thrift-plus-tailoring gets much closer, and the curation premium starts looking reasonable.

The Fit Question

One honest flag for both paths: fit. Re/Done’s rigid vintage fabric (100% cotton, no stretch) means fit can genuinely vary pair to pair — Nordstrom suggests considering one size up. Thrift has the same problem with no return policy. If you’re between sizes or hate sizing gambles, the “Originals” line (new stretch denim, more forgiving fit) exists — but at Re/Done prices for non-vintage denim, the value case weakens. For the vintage story, accept the rigid fabric and size carefully.

The Bottom Line

Re/Done vs. thrift-store vintage isn’t a scam-versus-smart story — it’s a time-versus-money story. The thrift store gives you the same authentic raw material for $20–$60 if you’ll do the hunting, the tailoring math, and the quality-control gamble yourself. Re/Done charges $265+ (less on sale) for the hunt done for you, the deconstruction and reconstruction, handmade LA construction, and a modern fit.

If you love the hunt, thrift and never look back. If you want vintage character with a tailored fit and zero weekends lost to racks, the premium is honest — especially on sale.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Re/Done just overpriced thrift-store jeans?

Not quite — the raw material is vintage Levi’s, but Re/Done deconstructs them completely and resews them against modern patterns in Los Angeles, with fit checks at each phase. You’re paying for curation, reconstruction labor, and a modern tailored fit, not just old jeans. Whether that labor is worth 5–10x the thrift price depends on how much you value your weekends.

Can I get the same look thrifting?

The authentic wear, yes — thrift-store vintage Levi’s have the same real decades-old fading for $20–$60. The modern tailored fit, no — vintage cuts run differently, and getting Re/Done’s High Rise Stovepipe silhouette from thrift requires tailoring luck. Thrift wins on price and authenticity of material; Re/Done wins on fit and convenience.

When is Re/Done actually worth it?

When you want vintage character with a modern fit without the hunt, and especially on sale — 40% off past-season washes isn’t unusual at retailers, which brings the premium down to reasonable territory. Against other designer denim ($250–$400), the pricing is in line; against thrift, you’re paying for curation and labor.

What’s the catch with Re/Done sizing?

Each pair is cut from different vintage jeans, so fit can vary — consider going one size up if you’re between sizes, per Nordstrom’s guidance. The vintage line is rigid 100% cotton with no stretch, so there’s no forgiveness. If you hate sizing gambles, try them in a store or look at the stretch-denim Originals line instead.
