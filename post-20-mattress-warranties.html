Mattress Warranties: What's Covered, What's Not, and the Fine Print

2026-10-07

A “10-year warranty” sounds reassuring — until you try to use it and discover that the sagging in the middle of your mattress doesn’t count, or that the stain from last year voided the whole thing. Mattress warranties are some of the most misunderstood documents in home goods, and the gap between what shoppers assume and what the warranty actually covers causes real frustration.

This guide explains what mattress warranties typically cover, what they exclude, how the fine print works, and how to protect your coverage from day one.

The Short Version

  • Warranties cover manufacturing defects — not comfort, not normal wear, not your changing preferences.
  • The key number is the sagging threshold: most warranties only cover impressions deeper than 1–1.5 inches (measured without weight on the mattress).
  • Stains void most warranties. Use a mattress protector from day one — this is the single most important warranty-protection step.
  • Proper foundation is required. Wrong base = voided warranty with most brands.
  • Warranty length (10, 15, 25 years) matters less than the coverage terms — read those, not the headline number.

What a Mattress Warranty Actually Covers

A warranty is a promise about defects in materials and workmanship. In practice, that means a short list of specific failures:

Covered (typically): - Sagging or impressions beyond the stated threshold (usually 1” to 1.5”, measured with no load) - Broken or loose coils in innerspring and hybrid mattresses - Split or cracked foam (not normal softening — actual structural failure) - Defective zippers, handles, or seams on the cover - Buniness or lumping from defective materials

Not covered (almost always): - Comfort preference. “It’s too firm” or “I don’t like it anymore” is what the trial period is for, not the warranty. - Normal softening. Foams soften with use — that’s expected, not defective. Only impressions beyond the threshold count. - Body impressions under the threshold. A 0.75” dip where you sleep is normal wear on most policies, even if you can feel it. - Damage from misuse, improper foundation, or accidents. - Stains, soiling, or bed bug treatment — these void most warranties outright.

The core idea: the warranty protects you against the mattress being built wrong, not against it wearing out or not suiting you.

The Sagging Threshold: The Number That Decides Everything

This is the single most important term in any mattress warranty, and most shoppers never read it.

Warranties don’t cover “my mattress sags.” They cover “my mattress has a measurable impression exceeding X inches.” That X is typically:

  • Budget mattresses: 1.5” threshold is common
  • Mid-range and premium: 1” threshold is common
  • Some luxury brands: 0.75” threshold

How it’s measured matters enormously. The standard method: lay a straight edge (like a broom handle or taut string) across the mattress surface with nothing on it, and measure the gap at the deepest point. Mattresses recover partially when unloaded — a dip that feels like 2” when you’re lying in it might measure 0.75” unloaded, which is under most thresholds.

This is why so many warranty claims are denied: the mattress genuinely feels worn out to the sleeper, but doesn’t meet the technical threshold. It’s also why foam density and coil quality matter at purchase — higher-quality materials resist impressions longer, keeping you above the threshold for more years.

The Fine Print That Voids Warranties

Stains. This is the big one. Most warranties state that any stain or soiling voids coverage — the logic being that stains suggest misuse or poor care, and they also make it impossible to resell or inspect the mattress. A single coffee spill in year 2 can void a 10-year warranty. A waterproof mattress protector costs a fraction of a mattress and eliminates this risk entirely.

Improper foundation. Most warranties require a specific type of support — a platform bed, an adjustable base, a proper box spring, or slats no more than a set distance apart (commonly 3”). Put a new mattress on your old sagging box spring and you’ve likely voided the warranty on day one. Check the foundation requirements before the mattress arrives.

Removing the law tag. The “do not remove” tag isn’t just a joke — some warranties require it to stay attached as proof of the model.

Commercial use. Using a residential mattress in a rental property, Airbnb, or other commercial setting typically voids the warranty.

Modifications. Cutting, bending (beyond what the design allows), or altering the mattress voids coverage.

Prorated vs. Non-Prorated: How the Math Works

Warranties come in two financial structures, and the difference is worth real money:

Non-prorated (full replacement): If the mattress fails within the warranty period, you get a repair or replacement at no cost. This is the better structure and it’s standard on most online-first brands’ 10-year warranties.

Prorated: After an initial full-coverage period (often 2–5 years), you pay a percentage of the replacement cost that increases each year. A failure in year 8 of a 10-year prorated warranty might cost you 60–80% of the current retail price — barely better than buying new.

Always check which structure applies, and for how many years the full-coverage portion lasts. A “20-year warranty” that’s prorated after year 3 is worse than a “10-year warranty” that’s fully non-prorated.

Warranty Length: What 10 vs 15 vs 25 Years Really Means

Longer sounds better, but the practical value depends on the terms:

  • 10 years is the industry standard for quality mattresses. Most mattresses are realistically replaced within 7–10 years anyway (see our guide on replacement signs).
  • 15–25 years usually comes with prorated structures after the first decade, stricter thresholds, or both. The extra years are mostly marketing.
  • What actually predicts longevity isn’t the warranty length — it’s build quality: foam density, coil gauge and count, and edge support. A well-built 10-year-warranty mattress typically outlasts a cheaply-built 20-year-warranty one.

How to File a Claim (and Get Approved)

If you have a legitimate defect:

  1. Measure properly. Straight edge across the surface, no load, photo with a tape measure showing the depth. Take photos from multiple angles.
  2. Check your foundation. Make sure your base meets the warranty requirements before you file — claims get denied on this.
  3. Check for stains. Be honest with yourself: any staining likely means denial. (This is why the protector matters.)
  4. Have your proof of purchase and law tag info ready.
  5. File through the retailer’s process — most now have online claim forms with photo upload.
  6. Be specific and factual. “1.25-inch impression measured unloaded at the center third, photos attached” beats “my mattress sags and my back hurts.”

Protecting Your Warranty From Day One

  • Waterproof mattress protector, fitted on day one, never removed except for washing.
  • Correct foundation — verify slat spacing or base type against the warranty terms.
  • Rotate the mattress per the manufacturer’s guidance (most modern mattresses are no-flip, but rotation helps even wear).
  • Keep your receipt and the law tag.
  • Read the actual warranty document — it’s usually a PDF on the product page, and it’s shorter than you think.

Warranty vs Trial vs Protection Plan: How They Stack

Shoppers often confuse three different protections. Here’s how they fit together:

Trial Period Warranty Protection Plan (sold separately)
Covers Comfort / fit (“I don’t like it”) Manufacturing defects Stains, accidents, sometimes more
Timeframe Nights to months (e.g., 100 nights) Years (e.g., 10 years) Varies (often 5 years)
Cost Free with purchase Free with purchase Extra purchase ($100–$300+)
Covers comfort changes? Yes — that’s its job No No
Covers stains? N/A No — stains void it Yes — that’s the main selling point

Do you need a paid protection plan? Honest answer: usually not, if you use a waterproof protector from day one. Protection plans primarily sell stain coverage — which a $30–$60 protector handles just as well. Where they can make sense: households with young kids or pets where accidents are near-certain, and you want the peace of mind in writing. Read the plan’s own fine print with the same skepticism — they have exclusions too.

The ideal stack: a clear trial period (for fit) + a solid manufacturer warranty (for defects) + a quality waterproof protector (for stains). That combination covers essentially everything a paid plan would, at a fraction of the cost.

Warranty FAQ

Does the warranty transfer if I sell or give away the mattress? Usually not. Most mattress warranties apply only to the original purchaser with proof of purchase. A few brands allow transfers — check the terms if you’re buying secondhand (though buying a used mattress has hygiene considerations beyond the warranty).

Does using an adjustable base void the warranty? Not with most modern brands — many explicitly allow adjustable bases, and some even require hinged/ flexible-friendly construction. But a minority of warranties (especially on traditional innersprings) exclude them. Verify in writing before pairing a new base with a new mattress.

What if I bought from a third-party retailer instead of direct? The manufacturer’s warranty generally still applies, but the claim process may route through the retailer. Keep the retailer’s receipt — the manufacturer will ask for it. Trial periods, however, are set by whoever sold it to you, and third-party trial terms are often worse than buying direct.

The Bottom Line

A mattress warranty is defect insurance, not comfort insurance and not a longevity promise. The headline year-count matters far less than three things: the sagging threshold, whether it’s prorated, and whether you’ve kept the conditions (protector, foundation, no stains) that keep it valid.

Buy the best-built mattress your budget allows, protect it from day one, and treat the warranty as a backstop — not a plan.
