If you wash your face with just your hands and cleanser, you’re doing okay. But plenty of people notice their skin never feels quite clean enough — makeup lingers along the hairline, pores feel congested by evening, and the skin just looks tired. That’s the problem a sonic facial brush promises to fix: deeper cleansing than hands alone, in the same sixty seconds you’d already spend washing.

TouchBeauty is one of the more affordable names in this space. They sell sonic facial cleansing brushes, facial massagers, and small skincare devices, and their products show up at retailers like Macy’s. But “affordable beauty device” is a category full of disappointments, so let’s look at what’s actually on offer before you spend anything.

What TouchBeauty Actually Is

TouchBeauty (styled TOUCHBeauty) is a beauty device brand that makes sonic and electric facial cleansing brushes, along with extras like eye massagers, microcurrent facial rollers, and heated massage wands. The core idea behind their brushes: sonic vibration pulses the bristles at high speed — some models claim over 22,000 vibrations per minute — to loosen dirt, oil, and makeup more effectively than fingers.

Key product details from real listings: the 2-in-1 Sonic Facial Brush Cleanser with Eye Massager retails around $29.99 at Macy’s, uses a water-drop shaped head for contouring, runs on batteries, and pairs the brush with a gentle eye massage attachment for puffiness. The rechargeable silicone models (like the B-1788 line) are IPX6 waterproof, use soft silicone bristles instead of nylon so there are no replacement heads to buy, and claim a battery life of up to 3 months per charge. Other devices in their line include a portable facial infusion massager (~$49.99) and a microcurrent facial roller (~$69.99).

Pricing sits firmly in the budget tier: roughly $25 to $70 depending on the model. Check the current price on their site before buying, since prices move.

Who It’s For — and Who Should Skip It

It’s for you if: you wear makeup daily and want a deeper cleanse without adding time to your routine; your skin tolerates gentle exfoliation; you want a cheap entry point into facial devices before committing to something like a Foreo; or you’re buying a simple skincare gift for someone starting a routine.

Skip it if: your skin is very sensitive or reactive, especially if you have rosacea or active acne — sonic brushes can irritate these conditions, and a dermatologist’s advice comes first. Also skip it if you already have a device you use happily, or if what you actually need is a better cleanser rather than a gadget. And if you’re looking for a premium, long-lasting device with replacement parts and a serious warranty, the budget price point tells you everything: this isn’t that tier of product.

Key Features Worth Knowing

  • Sonic vibration cleansing: High-frequency pulsing bristles claim to clean deeper than hands alone — the main selling point, and the one feature reviews consistently confirm works.
  • Dual-speed and brush combos: Some models offer two speeds and pair the cleanser with an eye-massager attachment, so one device covers face and under-eye.
  • Waterproof silicone models: The rechargeable silicone versions are IPX6 waterproof and don’t need replacement heads, which keeps the long-term cost at zero.
  • Battery convenience: The rechargeable models claim months of use per charge, so it’s not another device you’re constantly plugging in.
  • Budget-friendly pricing: Most of the lineup costs less than a single high-end cleanser, which is genuinely the brand’s strongest argument.

Price and Value

This is a budget beauty-tech brand, plain and simple. A facial brush in the $25–$35 range that works for a year or two is reasonable value — cheaper than most of a single fancy serum, and it gets daily use. The silicone, no-replacement-heads models are the better value of the two designs since there are no ongoing costs.

The flip side: at this price, you’re not getting premium build quality, and some models run on replaceable AAA batteries rather than recharging, which adds a small running cost. Set expectations accordingly — this is a solid tool, not a luxury one. And never buy a beauty device at full price without comparing it against the current listing, because pricing shifts often. The Bottom Line

TouchBeauty makes affordable sonic facial brushes that do what they promise: a deeper clean than your hands, with handy extras like eye massagers on some models. The silicone rechargeable versions are the ones to look at — waterproof, no replacement heads, months of battery per charge. It’s not premium gear, and it won’t rescue skin that needs a dermatologist, but for the price, it’s an honest product that earns its keep.
