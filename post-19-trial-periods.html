Mattress Trial Periods Explained: How to Actually Use Your 100 Nights

Most online mattresses now come with a “100-night trial” or even a full year to decide. It sounds generous — sleep on it for months, send it back if you don’t love it. But the fine print matters more than the headline number, and most shoppers waste the trial by judging the mattress in the first week, when their body hasn’t adjusted yet.

This guide explains how mattress trial periods actually work, what the common terms and exclusions are, and how to use the trial properly so you end up with the right mattress — whether that’s the one you bought or a better replacement.

The Short Version

  • A trial period is a return window, not a warranty. It covers comfort and fit, not defects or wear.
  • Most trials run 100 nights, though some brands advertise 120, 180, or 365 nights.
  • Nearly every trial has a mandatory break-in period (usually 21–30 nights) before you can start a return.
  • Returns are typically free — the company arranges pickup or donation — but always check the policy before buying.
  • Judge the mattress at night 30 and night 60, not night 3. Your body needs time to adjust.

What a Trial Period Actually Is

A sleep trial is a promise: buy the mattress, sleep on it in your own home, and if you don’t like it within the stated window, you can return it for a refund. It’s the online mattress industry’s answer to the showroom — since you can’t try before you buy, you try after you buy.

This is different from a Mattress Warranties: What’s Covered, What’s Not, and the Fine Print, which covers manufacturing defects over years. The trial covers one thing only: do you like sleeping on it?

Trials became standard because buying a mattress without lying on it felt risky. The data backs up the model — most people who keep a mattress past the break-in period keep it for years. The trial mostly protects the minority who got the firmness or feel wrong.

The Break-In Period: Why You Can’t Return on Day 5

Almost every trial policy includes a minimum ownership period — usually 21 to 30 nights — before you can initiate a return. This isn’t the company stalling you. There are two real reasons:

Your body needs to adjust. A new mattress changes your spinal alignment, pressure distribution, and sleep posture. The first week often feels “off” simply because it’s different, not because it’s wrong. Sleep researchers generally recommend at least 3–4 weeks before judging a new sleep surface.

The mattress needs to break in. Foams soften slightly with use. A mattress that feels a touch too firm on night 1 often feels just right by night 25 as the comfort layers settle. This is normal and expected — it’s why our Mattress Firmness Explained: How to Pick the Right Firmness for Your Sleep Position guide recommends erring slightly firm when you’re between options.

If you’re miserable at night 10, don’t panic — and don’t start the return yet. Give it the full break-in. But do start a simple sleep log (more on that below).

What “Free Returns” Really Means

Most major online brands advertise free returns, and for the most part that’s accurate — but “free” has a specific meaning here:

  • Pickup or donation. The company typically arranges for the mattress to be picked up from your home, or asks you to donate it locally and provide a receipt. You don’t have to compress it back into a box (which would be nearly impossible anyway).
  • Full refund. Most trials refund the full purchase price. Some deduct original shipping costs, especially on lower-priced models — check the policy.
  • No restocking fees is the norm for online-first brands, but not universal. Traditional retailers with trial programs sometimes charge them.

The exceptions to watch for:

  • Final-sale or clearance items are often excluded from trials entirely.
  • Some brands require you to keep the original packaging or charge a fee if it’s missing (rare, but it exists).
  • White-glove delivery orders (where the company sets up the mattress and removes your old one) sometimes have different return terms.
  • International orders may have limited or no return options.

Read the returns page before you buy, not after you’re unhappy. It takes five minutes and it’s the single highest-value reading you’ll do in the whole purchase.

How to Actually Use the Trial: A Week-by-Week Plan

Nights 1–7: Set up correctly, change nothing. Make sure the mattress is on a proper foundation — a bad base can make a good mattress feel terrible (see our guide to Box Spring vs Platform Bed when it’s published). Use your normal pillow and bedding. Don’t add a topper yet; you want to evaluate the mattress itself.

Nights 8–21: Keep a simple sleep log. Each morning, jot down three things: how long it took to fall asleep, whether you woke up with any aches, and a 1–10 sleep quality score. This takes 30 seconds and gives you real data instead of vague impressions.

Night 30: First real judgment. Review your log. Are mornings consistently better or worse than your old mattress? Is there a specific, repeatable problem (e.g., “my shoulder aches every morning” or “I sleep hot every night”)? Vague dissatisfaction at day 30 usually resolves; specific recurring pain usually doesn’t.

Nights 30–60: Troubleshoot before you quit. Many “wrong mattress” problems are actually accessory problems: - Shoulder or neck pain → often a pillow issue, not a mattress issue (see How to Choose a Pillow by Sleep Position). - Sleeping hot → check your bedding and room temperature before blaming the mattress (see Best Cooling Mattress for Hot Sleepers). - Partner disturbance → that’s a motion isolation issue and it’s a legitimate mattress problem (see Best Mattress for Couples).

Night 60–90: Make the call. If a specific problem has persisted through troubleshooting, start the return. Don’t wait until night 99 — give yourself buffer for pickup scheduling.

Trial Length: Does 365 Nights Beat 100?

Brands advertise longer trials as a selling point, and a longer window is slightly better for you — but the practical difference is smaller than it looks:

  • Most return decisions happen by night 60. If you haven’t decided by then, you probably like it well enough to keep it.
  • The break-in period is the same regardless of total length.
  • What matters more than length: free return shipping, no restocking fee, and a simple process. A hassle-free 100-night trial beats a 365-night trial with fine-print fees.

One thing a longer trial genuinely helps with: seasonal sleepers. If you sleep hot, buying in winter means you won’t test the mattress in heat until summer. A 365-night trial covers you across seasons; a 100-night trial might not.

Trial Periods and Price: What the Trial Costs the Company

Free trials aren’t free to offer — return shipping, pickup logistics, and donated mattresses cost real money, and it’s baked into the price. This is one reason budget mattresses (see How to Buy a Great Mattress on a Budget (Without Getting Scammed)) sometimes have shorter trials or stricter terms: there’s less margin to absorb returns.

It also explains why trials exist mostly in the online channel. A traditional store lets you lie on the mattress first, so the “risk reversal” job is already done. Online, the trial is the showroom.

Common Trial Mistakes

  1. Judging too early. Returning at night 12 because it “feels weird” wastes everyone’s time — including yours, since you’ll restart the adjustment clock on the next mattress.
  2. Adding a topper immediately. A mattress topper changes the feel completely. Evaluate the mattress alone first, then decide if you need one.
  3. Ignoring the foundation. A mattress on a sagging box spring or widely-spaced slats will feel unsupportive no matter how good it is.
  4. Waiting until the last week. Returns take time to process and schedule. Start at least 2–3 weeks before the deadline.
  5. Not reading the policy before buying. The time to learn about restocking fees and exclusions is before checkout, not at night 95.

After the Trial: What Happens on Night 101

Once the trial window closes, your safety net changes — the return option is gone, and the warranty becomes your only protection. A few things worth doing in the final week of the trial:

  • Make the decision actively. Don’t let the deadline pass by inertia. If you’re genuinely unsure at night 90 of a 100-night trial, that’s actually an answer — a mattress you loved wouldn’t leave you uncertain.
  • Save your documentation. Keep the order confirmation, the trial terms as they were when you bought (policies can change; screenshot them), and any correspondence. If a warranty issue arises in year 3, you’ll want proof of purchase date.
  • Register the warranty if required. Some brands require online registration within a set window. Do it now while the paperwork is in front of you.
  • Put the protector on if you haven’t. Warranty coverage from night 101 onward assumes a stain-free mattress. If you’ve been sleeping unprotected during the trial, start now.

One more scenario: second thoughts after the trial ends. If the trial has closed and you discover a real problem, you still have options — the warranty covers defects, and many credit cards extend return protection on purchases. It’s narrower than the trial, but it’s not nothing.

The Bottom Line

A trial period is the best consumer protection in the mattress industry — but only if you use it deliberately. Respect the break-in period, keep a simple log, troubleshoot accessories before blaming the mattress, and make your decision with buffer to spare.

Buy from a company with a clear, written trial policy (free returns, no restocking fee, stated break-in period), and you’ll have effectively zero risk. That’s the whole point: the trial exists so you can stop worrying and actually sleep.
